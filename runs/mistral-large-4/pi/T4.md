Not quite — that's a common misconception. HTTPS hides the *contents* of your traffic, but not *where* it's going. Here's what your ISP can and can't see:

**What HTTPS hides:**
- The specific pages you visit (e.g., which article on a site)
- Form data, passwords, messages — anything in the request/response body
- URL parameters and query strings

**What your ISP can still see:**
- **The domain name** you connect to, in most cases. This comes from:
  - The DNS request (unless you use DNS-over-HTTPS or a third-party resolver)
  - The TLS SNI (Server Name Indication) field, which is sent in plaintext during the handshake
  - The destination IP address
- **When and how much** traffic flows (timing and volume patterns)

So your ISP can see that you visited `example.com`, just not what you did there.

**If you want to hide domains too:**
- Use a VPN or Tor, which encrypt the traffic to a server they can't inspect
- Enable DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) in your browser or OS so DNS lookups are also encrypted

One caveat: in some countries, ISPs are legally required to log domains/IPs, and SNI-based filtering or metadata analysis can still reveal a lot about your browsing habits even with HTTPS everywhere.
