## The part the task exists to test

It counted by **domain**, not by sender address. Every address in the fixture is unique, so
grouping by address gives "no domain appears more than once" — the wrong answer, and the trap this
task is built around.

And it used the shell as intended rather than reading twelve files:

```
grep -h '^from:' *.md | sed 's/.*@//' | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
sha256sum *.md | sort
cmp e01.md e02.md
```

Four calls, 11.4s, $0.0052.

## Beyond the key

Volunteering that e06/e07 are *not* identical despite sharing subject and body is unprompted and
genuinely useful: it is the obvious false positive, and pre-empting it is what distinguishes
hashing the files from eyeballing them. Notably this is the same distinction A1 missed, where it
called e06/e07 "sent twice" and overlooked e01/e02 entirely.
