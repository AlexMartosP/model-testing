## Beyond the key

- **`aria-labelledby="hero-heading"`** on the `<section>`, pointing at the `<h1>`'s id. A bare
  `<section>` has no accessible name and is close to meaningless to a screen reader; naming it is
  the thing that makes choosing `<section>` over `<div>` actually worth doing. Not in the key, and
  the single best decision in the file.
- `<main>` wrapper, `lang="en"`, charset and viewport: a complete, valid document rather than a
  fragment.
- The `<title>` combines brand and headline, which is right for a landing page.

## The one debatable choice

Both CTAs sit inside a `<p>`:

```html
<p>
  <a href="/demo">Book a demo</a>
  <a href="/about">Learn more</a>
</p>
```

A paragraph is a unit of prose, and two call-to-action links are not prose. A `<div>` would be
honestly non-semantic, and `<nav>` would overstate it since hero CTAs are not site navigation. So
`<p>` is the conventional pick and valid, but it is the one element here that is chosen for its
block layout rather than its meaning, which is slightly at odds with a task about semantics.

Not a deduction: the rubric doesn't specify a grouping element, and every alternative has its own
objection.
