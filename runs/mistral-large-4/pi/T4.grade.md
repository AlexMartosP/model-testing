## The two problems

**1. "The DNS request (unless you use DNS-over-HTTPS **or a third-party resolver**)".**

As written this is wrong. Plain DNS over UDP/53 to 8.8.8.8 is still fully visible to the ISP in
transit — switching resolver changes who answers, not who can watch. Only encryption (DoH/DoT)
hides the query.

There is a defensible reading: using a third-party resolver does stop the ISP's *own* resolver
from logging your queries, which is a real and commonly-drawn distinction between ISP-as-resolver
and ISP-as-transit. But the sentence is listing things that stop the ISP *seeing* the request, and
a third-party resolver doesn't do that. Misleading as phrased, not fabricated — so a deduction,
not the invented-facts cap.

**2. The VPN advice has no caveat.** It says "Use a VPN or Tor, which encrypt the traffic to a
server they can't inspect." True of the ISP, but it omits what the key makes its own bullet: the
VPN provider can now see everything the ISP used to. On a privacy question, recommending a VPN
without that is the omission most likely to leave the reader worse informed than before, because
it reads as a solution rather than a transfer of trust. Tor is different in kind here and lumping
them together blurs it further.
